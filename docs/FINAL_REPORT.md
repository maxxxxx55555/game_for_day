# Heirs of Fortune 2.1.0 — final release report

Publication status: **2.1.0 is published and re-issued.** The publication gate returned PASS at source commit `466a8c164a70b89fbdfb1ef174cff98c0e323d06` (engine 4.7.stable.official.5b4e0cb0f, Windows x64, clean tree) on 2026-11-01, and the tag `v2.1.0` names that commit. The release carries exactly two assets, `HeirsOfFortune-Windows-2.1.0.zip` and `HeirsOfFortune-SHA256SUMS-2.1.0.txt`, whose digests are recorded below and in the acceptance certificate.

The edition was first published on 2026-10-30 from `807ba09` (archive SHA256 `c7199222573d17c8996d8774ffdd6238feef240753eaf803b137eba38b456226`, EXE `000b0eda79d7fa463a1e0e9ff64ebcfd976ad11b7fddd07dcccc01319e0fead9`, PCK `35ed80d81ef2b22712115af74ca0b99702c037536812f809020242f453652ca1`). That build is superseded, not deleted: it stays reproducible from `807ba09`, and its binaries are kept on the build host. It was re-issued because `main` had in the meantime taken the two-skin appearance system, so the published build and the published source disagreed about what 2.1.0 is. The earlier published release [2.0.1](https://github.com/maxxxxx55555/game_for_day/releases/tag/v2.0.1) is untouched, and the 2.0.2 candidate was never tagged or published.

Automated acceptance at the gate: strict compile over 38 shipping scripts with all 49 warning categories as errors; 50 headless and 51 rendered source cases in Russian and in English with zero failures; four exports; 12 exported-binary runs of 12, 9 of them victories, defeats and dynasty ledgers driven on the private QA export of the same source and 3 of them the delivered EXE refusing those routes with exit 64; 100 of 100 seeded cycles with 100 distinct final states in 1,664.4 s; 1.4.0 and 2.0.x profile round-trips; a 1,802.875-second rendered soak with 88 complete cycles, 1396 checkpoints, 1802 samples and zero failures. Total gate wall time 4,477.5 seconds. Full receipts: [certificate](ACCEPTANCE_CERTIFICATE.md), [kit](ANNOUNCE_KIT.md), [release notes](RELEASE_2.1.0.md), machine-readable set in `acceptance-2.1.0/`.

Balance: 112 scripted lives in 636.5 seconds under a conservative and an aggressive policy over the four birth origins, verdict PASS, with no dominant variant. Meteor reaches victory earlier than its siblings and pays for it in measured stress; heir pays a tenth of quarterly income for a larger inheritance and finishes later; steady is the reference. Perks and ascension levels shift age and stress without producing a free win.

Performance: measured against a 2.0.2 worktree in 6 and 6 interleaved attempts on this host with isolated save profiles. Steady-state frame cost is unchanged — median 16.669 ms against 16.665 ms and p95 17.253 ms against 17.259 ms — and the walk from intro to the first life card takes the same 13.0 processed frames in both, but 294.5 ms of wall time against 248.0 ms: about 46.5 ms of one-time presentation work on this host, which is the added screens being built, not a slower frame. The verdict the receipt gives is REVIEW: the harness allows 40 ms on that metric and this build overran it (dashboard_ready_ms +46.5 ms over the 40 ms allowance). The metric now reports frames and wall time separately because the earlier published pair (522 ms against 545.5 ms, three cold attempts) conflated the two and understated the difference; that claim is corrected here rather than carried forward. No every-device claim is made.

Presentation: re-rendered at `b7f894b` — 132 captured gallery frames across 136 slots in eight labelled contact sheets, both skins, both locales, both resolutions, plus 24 ambition rows; against the previous capture 7 of 16 dark-skin frames are byte-identical and the differing frames change only in live figures. 17 presentation defects were found and repaired, none open.

## Defects found and repaired while closing this edition

- QA capture folders were being packed into the shipped PCK; `export_presets.cfg` now excludes `builds/`, and the repository isolation check refuses a tracked path under it.
- The soak trend rule reported allocator oscillation at a few hundred megabytes of plateau as a leak; the allowance now scales to the plateau, and the post-warm-up window starts where the engine counts stop changing rather than at a fixed 120 s. Eight synthetic profiles are graded through the rule to prove it still fails 150 B/s, 1 KiB/s and ~6 MiB/run leaks, a never-settling object count and orphan nodes.
- The heir ambition was strictly dominant — upside with no cost. It now trades a tenth of quarterly income for its inheritance bonus, and both the model cost and the disclosure on the description the player reads are asserted by tests.
- Bulk document spacing had edited byte-pinned promise snapshots; the snapshots were restored verbatim and the promise inventory re-hashed, rather than loosening the promise test.
- The mechanics matrix carried an `execution_evidence` path that never existed in the repository; it now names the suites that actually run, and ambitions gained their own row with per-column assertions that exist.
- The appearance system's skin applier cleared every button font-size override, which widened the bottom bar past the 540 and 360 layouts and printed raw choice text over the choice cards; only stylebox overrides are cleared now.
- The dynasty ledger and the milestone card never repainted on a skin change, the appearance row in settings printed Russian item labels in the English build, and ledger and ambition copy used one hardcoded grey that vanished on the paper skin.
- The quote graph showed no unit, scale or axis until something resized it; the choice card measured its height against a width that no longer existed once the numeral gutter was added; the documented roman numerals were never drawn because no card received its index; the intro cover painted past its bounds over the caption; two new icons were cut at 1.5px instead of the documented 1.75px.
- A skin switch left everything that took a colour while its screen was built: the intro brand, the ambition hint and description, the outcome line, ledger rows and backdrop, currency and milestone surfaces kept the previous skin, while the outcome tones, the delta chips and the finale carried hardcoded hex copies of the dark tokens. Text colour is now named by token and repainted on switch, the literals are gone, and the finale is measured as an intentionally staged sheet rather than excused from the check.
- The credits overlay painted its backdrop once at build time and never listened for a skin change. Because the intro toggle is reachable by keyboard behind that overlay, switching skins with credits open left near-black body text on the previous dark sheet — a readability defect. The panel now repaints its own backdrop and the theme case drives exactly that path.

## Deferred after the independent re-check

The re-check of the repaint batch confirmed five closures and named three items that are recorded rather than silently fixed, because none of them changes what the shipped build shows a player and each would otherwise cost a full re-gate:

- `scripts/ReleaseVerification.gd` reads `dynasty_records[records_before]` after only appending a note when the hall gained no record, so a genuinely broken hall makes the route crash and the native runner fails by timeout instead of failing fast. The QA script is physically excluded from the public pack, so this affects diagnostic speed only.
- A comment above that route still claims a percentage-figure check that no longer exists; the disclosure is asserted on the description and the control tooltip instead, because the interface never shows the percentage strings.
- The reused boot splash keeps the theme it was created with if it is ever re-shown after being hidden; the unsaved dialog and the integrity notice were fixed at show time, this path was not.

Two limits of the new theme case are also stated rather than glossed: opening a ledger section calls `refresh()`, which rebuilds its rows, so the scan proves the rebuilt rows carry the active skin and cannot observe a row that kept an old colour; and the chip visibility check in the dynasty route reads the container's own `visible` flag, so it does not by itself catch a chip row that is visible but mid-reveal.
